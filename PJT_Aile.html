Glider Wing Design – Computational Fluid Dynamics Engineering Project

Complete aerodynamic and structural sizing of a high-performance glider wing using analytical methods, CFD (Siemens CCM+), and FEA (Abaqus).

1. Preliminary Design & Airfoil Selection [Image of Airfoil Geometry]

The initial phase involved defining the wing geometry to meet specific flight constraints: a mass of 500kg, a flight speed of 80km/h, and a total wingspan of 22m.

We compared two airfoil profiles, the RAF 27 and the CLARK Y. By calculating the Reynolds number (1.78 × 106) and analyzing Lift (Cz) vs. Drag (Cx) polars, we determined that the Clark Y airfoil offered superior performance.

The Clark Y was selected for its higher maximum lift-to-drag ratio (fmax = 115 vs 70) and a more favorable stall angle (14.7°), ensuring better aerodynamic efficiency and safety margins.

Download Preliminary Report

2. CFD Simulation & Mesh Strategy

To validate the aerodynamic coefficients, we performed Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) simulations using Siemens CCM+. A critical part of this study was the mesh convergence analysis, ensuring that the results were independent of grid density.

We tested three flow regimes: inviscid, laminar, and turbulent. For the turbulent flow, we selected the K-Epsilon model due to its robustness and stability in convergence compared to K-Omega.

The simulation allowed us to visualize the pressure field and velocity vectors, confirming the generation of lift and the presence of a turbulent wake at the trailing edge.

Download CFD/Mesh Report

3. Structural Analysis & Finite Element Modeling [Image of Finite Element Analysis]

The final phase focused on the mechanical integrity of the wing. We utilized both analytical Euler-Bernoulli beam models and Abaqus FEA simulations to size the internal spar.

We conducted a parametric study comparing a hollow circular spar against a rectangular spar. The rectangular configuration using Aluminum 7075-T6 was proved to be the optimal solution, satisfying all constraints:
• Max deflection < 50cm
• Max stress < Elastic Limit (505 MPa)
• Minimized mass.

Abaqus simulations confirmed that the rectangular spar maintained structural stability under both ground (static) and flight (dynamic) loading conditions.
